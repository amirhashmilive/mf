01 · Overview

Geography before scale

Eight districts, all within a day's drive of Dhamtari. This is deliberate. We do not plan geographic expansion; we plan depth expansion — more villages, more rivers, more schools within the same eight districts. Continuity is what makes the work compound.

Contiguous geography

All eight districts share borders. A field officer in Dhamtari can be in Bastar by lunchtime. Proximity is a programme design choice, not an accident.

Dhamtari as HQ

Dhamtari has been our home and headquarters since 2011. It carries the densest programme footprint — Bolti Nadi, Farmours, Prayaas, Yoga Kutumb, Heritage all run here.

Annual re-verification

Each district profile is re-verified annually against SHG registers, school records, and FPO procurement data. Inactive villages roll off the count; new ones are added.

Why eight, not eighty?

A rural NGO's most precious asset is trust, and trust compounds with continuity. The same field officer visiting the same village for the fourth year can have a conversation that the same officer could not have had in year one. Eight districts is the largest area we can hold with that depth. Eighty districts would be a different organisation — and a worse one.

04 · Awareness

Why districts, not states

The district is the unit at which Indian rural development actually happens. State-level aggregates hide district-level reality. These slides explain the principles that govern our geographic strategy.

Geographic principles
8
Depth before breadth
Districts

We work in eight districts — not eighty. Depth is the multiplier on rural trust, and trust is the only thing that compounds.

Walkable

Every district is close enough to be walked by the same field team. Walking is method, ethics, and accountability.

Annual re-verify

Each district profile is rebuilt annually. Inactive villages roll off; new ones are added. No silent rounding.

Trust compounds

Year-four conversations in the same village are different from year-one conversations. Continuity is the asset.

06 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Three filters. (1) Contiguity — the new district must border an existing one, so the same field team can hold the whole map. (2) Invitation — we enter a district only when invited by a credible local partner (a gram sabha, an ashram school, a district administration). (3) Capacity — we add a district only when the existing districts are stable enough to spare field-team time. Kawardha (2023) was the most recent addition; we typically add one district every two to three years.
