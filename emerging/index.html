01 · Overview

Rural Chhattisgarh, in its own voice

Not every village has a newspaper. Not every farmer reads the news. But every village has a radio, every child can draw, and every field team has a story worth telling. Our emerging platforms exist because the communities we work with have always spoken — we are simply handing them a microphone.

Why emerging media?

When literacy is low, internet is patchy, and television is rare, community-owned media becomes the most reliable way to share information, awareness, and stories from the ground.

Community-owned

Every platform is led by community reporters — village youth, women's SHG members, and school children trained by our team. We don't broadcast at them; we broadcast with them.

Four platforms

Community radio (Meer FM 90.4), media gallery, news & press desk, and field-notes blog — each tailored to a different audience, each faithful to the same villages.

03 · Awareness

Why rural media matters

Community media is not nostalgia — it is infrastructure. When a village has its own radio, its own children's magazine, and its own reporters, information stops being something done to the village and becomes something the village does for itself.
