01 · Overview

A magazine where children hold the pen

Guriya — 'doll' in Chhattisgarhi — is Meer Foundation's quarterly children's magazine. Eighty per cent of every issue is written and illustrated by children aged 8 to 16, drawn from Prayaas learning centres, government schools, and tribal ashram schools across 8 districts of Chhattisgarh.

Why a magazine?

A magazine gives a child the rare gift of being read. When their stories appear in print, beside their name and their school, the dignity of authorship changes how they see themselves — and how their village sees them.

Trilingual, natively

Every issue carries Chhattisgarhi, Hindi, and English side by side. None is a translation of the other — each is written natively, preserving the rhythm of the language the child thinks in.

Curriculum-aligned

Guriya is mapped to the CGBSE language and art curricula. Teachers receive a companion guide with lesson prompts for each issue, so the magazine lives inside the classroom, not only at home.

02 · Four issues · 2026

Four issues, four themes

Every year, Guriya follows the same four-theme arc — Bachpan, Nadi, Kisan, Virasat. Each theme draws on a different strand of a child's life in rural Chhattisgarh: their childhood, their river, their farmer, their heritage. Children may submit to any or all issues.

03 · Features

What makes Guriya different

Four design principles guide every issue. Together they make Guriya unlike any other children's magazine in India — not because it is more polished, but because it is more honest.

Trilingual

Chhattisgarhi · Hindi · English — all three in every issue, side by side. No language is a translation of the other; each is written natively.

Quarterly

Four issues per year, one per quarter. Each issue aligned to a season and a community theme — Bachpan, Nadi, Kisan, Virasat.

Children-authored

80% of every issue is written and illustrated by children aged 8–16. Our editors guide, never rewrite. Children retain bylines.

Curriculum-aligned

Tied to CGBSE language and art curricula. Teachers can use Guriya in classrooms — every issue includes a teacher's note.

04 · Submit your work

How to submit

Any child from any school in Chhattisgarh can submit to Guriya. Selected contributors receive a printed copy of the issue they appear in, a certificate of authorship, and a byline inside the magazine. Submissions are accepted in Chhattisgarhi, Hindi, or English.

01

Pick a theme

Each quarter has a theme — Bachpan, Nadi, Kisan, Virasat. Submissions must respond to the upcoming issue's theme.

02

Create your work

Stories, poems, essays, drawings, or photo essays. Word limit 800 words; art should be A4-size scans at 300 DPI.

03

Submit by deadline

Email to guriya@meerfoundation.org with subject 'Q[X] submission — [child's name], [school]'. Deadlines: 1 Feb, 1 May, 1 Aug, 1 Nov.

04

Get published

Selected contributors receive a printed copy of the issue and a certificate. Top entries are featured on the inside front cover.

05 · Awareness

Why a children's magazine matters

A magazine is not just reading material — it is a small, physical assertion that a child's voice belongs in print. In rural Chhattisgarh, where children's publishing in Chhattisgarhi is almost non-existent, Guriya is a quiet act of cultural repair.
