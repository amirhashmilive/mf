01 · Overview

The story behind Meer Foundation

Before Meer Foundation was a 12-district operation, it was a question: how do we know that an institution's work is actually making the communities it touches measurably better? Fourteen years later, that question remains the foundation's North Star.

The question

How does an institution prove — to a skeptical observer — that its presence has measurably improved the communities it serves? This is the founding question.

The answer

Working with people, never for them. Building architecture that demonstrates outcomes, not activity. Instilling agency, not dependency.

The evidence

15 years. 1 lakh+ households. 860+ SHGs. 90 km river walked. 12 districts. Every programme mapped to the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

02 · The Founder

Amir Hashmi — architect of the Five Pillars

Formally Dr. Sayed Amir Mustafa Hashmi, the founder's work across rural Chhattisgarh — from the 90-kilometre Sakri River Walk to the formation of 860+ women's Self-Help Groups — is the empirical foundation of the Five Pillars Framework.

Amir Hashmi founded Meer Foundation in 2011 with a single conviction: institutions must be able to demonstrate, with evidence that would convince a skeptical observer, that the communities they touch are measurably better because of their presence.

His work across Chhattisgarh — from the 90-kilometre Sakri River Walk to the formation of 860+ women's Self-Help Groups — is the empirical foundation of the Five Pillars Framework, a communication framework for transparent, evidence-based institutional communication that he sets out in his book of the same name.

Under his leadership, Meer Foundation has reached over one lakh households across twelve districts of Chhattisgarh, with a focus on marginalised Dalit and Tribal communities. The foundation's work is consistently aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

Vision

Self-reliant rural communities across Chhattisgarh — built through education, environment, health, livelihood, and women empowerment, all measured against the Betterment Question.

Method

Recruit professionals with diverse expertise and a commitment to empathy. Prioritise skill development within communities rather than imposing solutions. Instil agency and self-determination.

03 · Timeline

From a 2011 registration to a 12-district operation

Fourteen years of work, told in seven inflection points. Each entry is verifiable — through annual reports, registration documents, field reports, and press coverage.

2011December

The Foundation is registered

On 23 December 2011, Meer Foundation is registered under the Chhattisgarh Society Registration Act 1973 (44), Reg. No. 3588, in Dhamtari. Amir Hashmi begins with a single conviction: institutions must be able to demonstrate, with evidence, that the communities they touch are measurably better because of their presence.

2013Field years

Listening, not delivering

The first two years are spent not on programmes but on listening. Field teams walk through tribal hamlets in Dhamtari, Kanker, and Bastar — documenting what households actually need, not what an institution assumed they needed. This is the empirical seed of the Five Pillars Framework.

2016Monsoon

Bolti Nadi — let the river speak

Meer Foundation launches Bolti Nadi, the Save River Initiative, with a single premise: a river that cannot speak for itself must be given a voice. The mission is to make rural Chhattisgarh drought-free through community-led river restoration.

2019August

Sakri River Walk + Tiranga World Record

A 90-kilometre on-foot expedition along the Sakri River documents water quality, agricultural dependence, and ecosystem conditions. The report triggers riverbank plantation, check dams, and a sewage treatment plant. Days earlier, on 11 August, the foundation partners in a 15-km human chain — the Meri Jaan Tiranga Hai rally in Raipur.

2020Pandemic onward

Farmours — from subsistence to enterprise

Through the pandemic and beyond, Meer Foundation scales Farmours — empowering 12,000+ women farmers through Self-Help Groups, Farmer Producer Organisations, and grassroots governance training. 860+ SHGs and 35 block-level federations take root across 12 districts.

2024July

12A, 80G, CSR-1 secured

Section 12A (AACAM4289JE20241) and 80G (AACAM4289JF20241) are approved on 08-07-2024. CSR-1 (CSR00075824) follows on 16-07-2024. Meer Foundation is now eligible for CSR funding and 80G tax-deductible donations — a 13-year compliance journey completed.

TodayYear 14

1 lakh+ households · 12 districts · 7 initiatives

Meer Foundation today reaches over one lakh households across twelve districts of Chhattisgarh, with seven active initiatives spanning education, environment, health, livelihood, women empowerment, research, and emerging media. Every programme is governed by the Five Pillars Framework (A-E-I-O-M) and aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.
