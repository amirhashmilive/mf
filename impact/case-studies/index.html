01 · Overview

Stories that the numbers cannot tell

Statistics tell you how many. Case studies tell you how. Each of the five cases below documents a different facet of the Meer Foundation practice — a river walked, a woman-led FPO built, a tribal district taught, a cohort studied, a world record set.

Three-layer verification

Each case is signed off by (1) the community it describes, (2) an internal Meer Foundation reviewer who did not work on the case, and (3) where applicable, a government or academic reviewer.

Geographic specificity

Every case carries a real location, a real year, and a real village list. No anonymised clusters. No composite narratives.

Outcome over output

A bridge built is an output. Crossings made is an outcome. We count outcomes. Each case ends with a metric that means something to the people it describes.

How each case is built

A case study begins as a field report. It is then expanded through interviews with named community members, cross-checked against SHG registers / school records / PHC data, returned to the village for a community meeting, and finally reviewed by an internal Meer Foundation reviewer who did not work on the case. The footer of each case lists every contributing village and every reviewer.

02 · Five case studies

From Sakri to Tiranga — five verified stories

Sakri River Restoration · Dhamtari, Chhattisgarh

Photo: 2019 – 2024

EnvironmentBolti Nadi
2019 – 2024· Dhamtari, Chhattisgarh

Sakri River Restoration

Over five years, the Bolti Nadi team walked all 90 km of the Sakri river system end-to-end on foot, meeting every riparian village along the way. The walk produced a primary dataset on bank erosion, sand mining, and riparian forest loss — and a restoration plan co-authored by 12 villages.

Verified outcome

Native riparian plantation restored along 14 km of riverbank. Sand-mining permits in two stretches were paused by the district administration after the team submitted its DO and turbidity data. Twelve villages adopted a joint river-management gram-sabha resolution.

6Clean Water15Life on Land
90 km walked · 12 villages · 14 km restored

Sunita's FPO · Dhamtari, Chhattisgarh

Photo: 2021 – 2025

LivelihoodFarmours
2021 – 2025· Dhamtari, Chhattisgarh

Sunita's FPO

Sunita Verma, a 34-year-old farmer from Mohdi village, was one of the first women trained under Farmours' organic-farming curriculum. In four years she built — and now chairs — a women-led Farmer Producer Organisation spanning 3,320 households across Dhamtari and Kanker.

Verified outcome

The FPO reported an average household income of ₹2.5 lakh in FY 2024–25, verified against procurement records and bank deposits. Sunita now mentors 18 newer FPOs across the state, and the Dhamtari model is being studied by the State Rural Livelihoods Mission.

3,320 households · ₹2.5L income · 18 FPOs mentored

Prayaas Bastar · Bastar, Chhattisgarh

Photo: 2022 – 2026

EducationPrayaas
2022 – 2026· Bastar, Chhattisgarh

Prayaas Bastar

Prayaas, our free CGBSE-aligned virtual school, was extended to Bastar in 2022. The programme delivered Class 1–12 study materials in Hindi and English to 62 tribal ashram schools — many of which had no regular subject teachers for mathematics, science, or English.

Verified outcome

Class 10 pass percentage in the 12 Prayaas-supported Bastar schools rose from 41% (2022) to 78% (2025). Six Prayaas alumni are now in the first cohort of the EduSuTo coaching track, preparing for state engineering and nursing entrance exams.

4Quality Education10Reduced Inequalities
62 classrooms · 41% → 78% pass · 6 alumni in coaching

Yoga Kutumb Cohort · Dhamtari & Kanker, Chhattisgarh

Photo: 2023 – 2025

HealthYoga Kutumb
2023 – 2025· Dhamtari & Kanker, Chhattisgarh

Yoga Kutumb Cohort

A two-year cohort study followed 240 rural households through the Yoga Kutumb programme — weekly pranayama, asana, and emergency-response training. The cohort was matched against a control group from neighbouring villages.

Verified outcome

After 24 months, the cohort reported 38% fewer hospital visits for cardiac and respiratory complaints (self-reported, verified against PHC registers). Eighty-five per cent of households said they had used the emergency-response training at least once in the period.

3Good Health & Well-being
240 households · 38% fewer visits · 85% used training

Tiranga World Record · Raipur, Chhattisgarh

Photo: 12 August 2019

HeritageTiranga
12 August 2019· Raipur, Chhattisgarh

Tiranga World Record

On the eve of Independence Day 2019, Meer Foundation convened a 15-kilometre continuous human chain in Raipur — the 'Meri Jaan Tiranga Hai' rally. Citizens of every age, religion, and language held the tricolour end-to-end from Tatibandh to Telibandha.

Verified outcome

The rally entered the Limca Book of Records as the longest continuous human chain holding the national flag. Coverage ran in The Hindu, Times of India, and Dainik Bhaskar. The founder addressed the closing ceremony at Telibandha lake.

16Peace, Justice & Institutions
15 km chain · 1 world record · 3 national outlets

03 · Awareness

Why case studies — and why only five

A case study is not a victory lap. It is a method. These slides explain the principles that decide which stories get told, and which stay in the field notebook.

Case-study principles
3
Community, internal, external
Layers

Each case is verified three ways: by the community it describes, by an internal Meer Foundation reviewer who did not work on it, and where applicable by a government or academic partner.

Field-first

Every case begins as a field report — written by the team that walked the river, formed the FPO, or taught the class.

Community-signed

Before publication, the case returns to the village for a gram-sabha reading. Disagreements are recorded in the appendix.

Outcome-metric

Each case closes with one metric that means something to the people it describes — not a vanity number.

05 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Three criteria. (1) The case must have a verified outcome — not just an output. (2) The community behind the case must be willing to be named and to sign off on the narrative. (3) The case must be replicable — there must be something another village, district, or NGO could learn from it. We publish roughly two to three new case studies per year; the bar is high on purpose.
