01 · Overview

Learn. Live. Lead. Transform Chhattisgarh.

The Meer Foundation Development Apprenticeship Program (MDAP) is a structured, immersive, field-based learning programme designed to build the next generation of rural development professionals. Apprentices work across Meer Foundation's four practice areas — Education, Health, Environment, and Livelihood — gaining hands-on experience in real programmes serving real communities across Chhattisgarh.

Why MDAP exists. Rural development cannot be learned in a classroom alone. It requires field immersion, community trust, and the discipline of evidence-based practice. MDAP bridges the gap between academic study and professional practice — producing development professionals who are grounded, ethical, and effective.

02 · Why Join MDAP?

Why join MDAP?

MDAP is not an internship in the traditional sense. It is a professional apprenticeship designed to produce development practitioners who can design, execute, and evaluate rural development work from day one.

Four-sector exposure

Work across Education, Health, Environment, and Livelihood — not siloed in one sector. Understand how development is interconnected.

Real field placement

Live and work in a Chhattisgarh district. Not a visit — a placement. You become part of the community for the duration.

1:1 senior mentorship

A senior programme lead is your mentor. Weekly check-ins. Structured feedback at 30, 60, and 90 days. They sign your certificate.

Capstone portfolio piece

Your capstone project — a programme brief, research report, or community plan — becomes a real portfolio piece you can show future employers.

Competency-based certificate

Your certificate cites specific competencies and deliverables. Not an attendance certificate — a professional credential.

Career pathway

Several current Meer Foundation team members are former apprentices. MDAP is the most reliable pathway into a full-time development role.

03 · Who Can Apply

Who can apply?

MDAP is open to students, recent graduates, and early-career professionals from a wide range of academic disciplines. We value diversity — it strengthens the cohort and the work.

Eligibility criteria

  • 18 years or older
  • Enrolled in or completed an undergraduate or postgraduate programme
  • Minimum 2-month commitment (summer intensive)
  • Willingness to live and work in rural Chhattisgarh
  • Basic proficiency in Hindi (Chhattisgarhi is a bonus, not required)
  • No prior rural experience mandatory

Don't see your discipline? If your background is relevant to rural development, we encourage you to apply. We evaluate applicants on motivation and fit, not on the name of the degree.

05 · Learning Model

Four-phase learning model

MDAP follows a structured four-phase learning model that takes apprentices from orientation to capstone. Each phase builds on the previous — theory informs practice, practice informs reflection.

Weeks 1–2
Phase 1

Orientation & Foundations

Introduction to Meer Foundation's mission, the Five Pillars Framework, and the Chhattisgarh development context. Sessions on rural development ethics, community engagement protocols, and field safety.

Key activities
  • Five Pillars Framework immersion
  • Chhattisgarh socio-economic context
  • Community engagement ethics
  • Field safety and protocols
  • Team introductions and mentor matching
Weeks 3–6
Phase 2

Classroom Learning

Structured learning modules covering programme design, monitoring & evaluation, community-based research, and communication for development. Delivered through seminars, case studies, and assigned readings.

Key activities
  • Programme design and theory of change
  • Monitoring & evaluation fundamentals
  • Community-based research methods
  • Communication for development
  • Case study analysis and presentations
Weeks 7–16+
Phase 3

Field Placement

Apprentices are placed within one of Meer Foundation's initiatives across a Chhattisgarh district. This is the core of the programme — hands-on, supervised, community-facing work.

Key activities
  • Initiative-specific fieldwork
  • Community engagement and interviews
  • Data collection and documentation
  • Mentor-supervised deliverables
  • Weekly field reports and reflection
Final 2–4 weeks
Phase 4

Capstone & Evaluation

Apprentices complete a capstone deliverable — a project brief, a research report, a community plan, or a programme evaluation. Presented to a panel and evaluated against the competency framework.

Key activities
  • Capstone project development
  • Panel presentation and defence
  • Competency evaluation
  • Exit interview and feedback
  • Certification and alumni onboarding

Phase progression. Phases are sequential — each builds on the previous. The orientation phase is non-negotiable. The field placement is the core. The capstone is where you prove what you have learned.

07 · Competency Framework

Competency framework

MDAP apprentices are evaluated against a six-competency professional framework. Each competency is assessed at three levels — developing, proficient, and advanced — through mentor evaluation, peer feedback, and capstone defence.

Programme Design

Ability to design a development programme using theory of change and logical framework approaches.

Community Engagement

Skill in engaging rural communities respectfully, conducting interviews, and facilitating meetings.

Research & Analysis

Competence in community-based research, data collection, analysis, and report writing.

Monitoring & Evaluation

Understanding of M&E frameworks, indicators, data collection tools, and outcome measurement.

Communication

Ability to communicate development work through reports, presentations, stories, and visual media.

Field Ethics

Commitment to ethical practice, community consent, data privacy, and institutional accountability.

LevelMeaning
DevelopingApprentice understands the competency and can apply it with significant mentor support.
ProficientApprentice can apply the competency independently in familiar contexts.
AdvancedApprentice can apply the competency in novel contexts and mentor others.

09 · Field Assignments

Field assignments

During the field placement phase, apprentices work on real assignments that contribute to Meer Foundation's programmes. These are not simulated exercises — they are real work with real consequences for real communities.

  • 1Conducting community needs assessments in assigned villages
  • 2Supporting the design and delivery of an initiative-specific intervention
  • 3Collecting and analysing field data for monitoring and evaluation
  • 4Documenting case studies and beneficiary stories
  • 5Assisting in the organisation of community events and workshops
  • 6Contributing to research reports and publications
  • 7Supporting proposal development and donor reporting
  • 8Participating in stakeholder meetings and government liaison

Field placement locations. Assignments are carried out in districts of Chhattisgarh where Meer Foundation is active — Dhamtari, Raipur, Kanker, Bastar, Balod, Balodabazar, Kawardha, and Bemetara. Accommodation is arranged for placements requiring relocation.

10 · Evaluation & Certification

Evaluation & certification

MDAP evaluation is competency-based, not attendance-based. Apprentices are assessed at three checkpoints and must successfully defend a capstone project to receive certification.

30-day checkpoint

Foundation check

Mentor evaluates understanding of the Five Pillars Framework, community engagement ethics, and initiative context.

60-day checkpoint

Mid-term review

Mentor and programme team evaluate field performance, deliverable progress, and competency development.

Final checkpoint

Capstone defence

Apprentice presents capstone project to a panel. Competency framework evaluated. Certificate issued on pass.

11 · Career Pathways

Career pathways

MDAP is designed as a launchpad for a career in rural development. While we cannot guarantee a job, the apprenticeship opens doors — at Meer Foundation and beyond.

Full-time at Meer Foundation

Several current team members are former apprentices. Performance during MDAP is the strongest signal for full-time fit.

Other NGOs & foundations

MDAP certificate is recognised by CSR partners and development organisations across India. Your capstone is your portfolio.

Higher education

MDAP field experience strengthens applications for development studies, public policy, and social work programmes.

Social entrepreneurship

Some alumni have launched their own initiatives. MDAP provides the field knowledge and network to start.

12 · Who Should Apply

Who should apply?

MDAP is looking for people who are serious about rural development as a career — not just a CV line. If any of these describe you, we want to hear from you.

You are driven by purpose

You want your work to matter to real communities. You are willing to trade comfort for impact.

You are curious and adaptable

You ask questions before offering answers. You can work in unfamiliar contexts without losing your footing.

You value evidence over opinion

You ground your conclusions in data and community input. You are willing to change your mind.

You respect communities

You see communities as partners, not beneficiaries. You listen before you speak.

You are committed to growth

You accept feedback. You want to be stretched. You are not looking for an easy certificate.

You are ready for the field

You are willing to live in rural Chhattisgarh. You understand that fieldwork is the heart of development.

13 · Programme Values

Programme values

These four values are the non-negotiable foundation of MDAP. They guide how we work, how we evaluate, and how we certify. If they resonate with you, you will thrive here.

Empathy before expertise

We listen before we speak. We learn before we teach. Communities are partners, not subjects.

Evidence over opinion

We ground our work in data, community input, and the Five Pillars Framework. Proclamation is not proof.

Agency over dependency

We build capacity, not dependence. Communities drive their own development; we walk alongside.

Field over office

We work where the work happens. The field is our classroom, our laboratory, and our constituency.
