Overview

A 14-year arc of field-first research

Meer Foundation's research arm exists for one reason: to close the Legitimacy Gap — the gap between what institutions claim and what people experience. We do this through on-foot fieldwork, mixed-methods rigour, and an open-access-by-default publishing policy. Our research is not the kind that gathers dust in a journal. It is the kind a sarpanch can cite in a gram sabha.

Methodology

How we work: six non-negotiables

Method is what separates research from advocacy. These six principles govern every Meer Foundation research output — from the first river walk to the final DOI.

On-foot fieldwork

Researchers walk the river. We do not write about rivers from a desk in Raipur; we walk them, village by village, bend by bend.

Mixed-methods rigour

Hydrological sampling, ecological transects, oral histories, household surveys, and satellite imagery triangulate every claim.

Five Pillars lens

Every research output is interrogated through the Five Pillars Framework — the communication lens for transparent institutional communication.

Open access by default

All reports are released under CC-BY 4.0. No paywalls, no embargoes, no permissions required for non-commercial use.

Citation-grade outputs

Every report carries a DOI where applicable, full references, methodology appendix, and raw data annexure.

Community-reviewed

Before publication, drafts are returned to the villages and stakeholders who appear in them. Their assent is required.

Field-first

Every research question begins on the ground, not in a literature review.

Triangulated

Quantitative, qualitative, and oral-history sources cross-check every claim.

Reviewed

Peer review at the journal level; community review at the field level.
