Course Modules

10-week curriculum — click any module to expand full content

Module 1: Climate Drivers
Weeks: 1–2 | Topics: 7 | Grade: 10% | Time: ~18 hrs
1.1 The Greenhouse Gas Effect

The natural greenhouse effect maintains Earth's average temperature at +15°C. Human activities have raised atmospheric CO&sub2; from 280 ppm (pre-industrial) to 420+ ppm today, intensifying this effect and driving +1.1°C of observed warming.

  • Major greenhouse gases: CO&sub2;, CH&sub4;, N&sub2;O, F-gases
  • Radiative forcing, feedback loops, and tipping points
1.2 The Paris Agreement & International Policy

The Paris Agreement (2015) established the 1.5°C and 2°C temperature goals. Countries submit Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) every 5 years.

  • NDC structure: mitigation targets, adaptation plans, finance needs
  • Article 6.2 and Article 6.4 explained
1.3 The Carbon Budget

The remaining 1.5°C carbon budget is approximately 250 GtCO&sub2; — at current global emissions, this will be exhausted in ~6 years.

1.4 Global Energy Systems

Fossil fuels provide 81% of global primary energy. The energy transition requires simultaneous renewable expansion and CCS for hard-to-abate sectors.

1.5 Introduction to Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS)

CCS captures 90–99% of CO&sub2; from industrial point sources, compresses it, transports it, and injects it into deep geological formations.

1.6 Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR) Technologies

Nature-based options (afforestation, biochar) and Technology-based options (BECCS, DAC).

1.7 Module 1 Assessment

Assignment: Carbon Budget Analysis (500 words).
Quiz: 10 MCQs covering all Module 1 topics.

Module 2: CCS Across the Economy
Weeks: 3–4 | Topics: 6 | Grade: 20% | Time: ~22 hrs
2.1 Industrial Point-Source Capture

Cement, steel, and chemicals account for roughly 20% of global CO&sub2; emissions. CCS is often the only viable decarbonisation pathway for these sectors due to process emissions.

2.2 Power Sector Decarbonisation

Retrofitting coal and gas power plants with CCS to provide dispatchable, low-carbon electricity.

2.3 Capture Technologies

Post-combustion (amines), pre-combustion (gasification), and oxyfuel combustion technologies.

2.4 Blue Hydrogen Production

Producing hydrogen from natural gas with CCS (Steam Methane Reforming or Autothermal Reforming).

2.5 Direct Air Capture (DAC)

Extracting CO&sub2; directly from the atmosphere using liquid solvents or solid sorbents.

2.6 Module 2 Assessment

Assignment: Techno-economic analysis of a capture facility.
Quiz: 10 MCQs.

Module 3: Technologies for Capturing Carbon & NBS
Weeks: 5–6 | Topics: 8 | Grade: 20% | Time: ~24 hrs
3.1 Nature-Based Solutions Overview

Harnessing natural ecosystems to draw down CO&sub2;.

3.2 Soil Carbon Sequestration

Regenerative agriculture practices to build soil organic matter.

3.3 Biochar Production and Application

Converting agricultural waste into stable carbon (biochar) through pyrolysis.

3.4 Agroforestry Systems

Integrating trees into agricultural landscapes for carbon storage and co-benefits.

3.5 Biomass Energy with CCS (BECCS)

Combining bioenergy with geological storage for net-negative emissions.

3.6 Smallholder Aggregation Models

Structuring carbon projects across thousands of smallholder farmers.

3.7 Digital MRV for Agriculture

Using satellite data, IoT, and mobile apps to measure nature-based carbon.

3.8 Module 3 Assessment

Assignment: NBS Project Design.
Quiz: 10 MCQs.

Module 4: Geological Storage & Permanence
Weeks: 7–8 | Topics: 7 | Grade: 15% | Time: ~18 hrs
4.1 Deep Saline Aquifer Storage

Injecting CO&sub2; into deep, porous rock formations containing saltwater.

4.2 Depleted Oil and Gas Reservoirs

Utilising known geological structures for permanent storage.

4.3 CO&sub2; Transport Infrastructure

Pipeline networks and shipping logistics for liquid CO&sub2;.

4.4 Trapping Mechanisms

Structural, residual, solubility, and mineral trapping over 10,000+ years.

4.5 Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV)

4D seismic, pressure monitoring, and geochemical tracers to ensure permanence.

4.6 Leakage Risk Assessment

Evaluating and mitigating risks of CO&sub2; migration.

4.7 Module 4 Assessment

Assignment: Site Assessment Report.
Quiz: 10 MCQs.

Module 5: Future Prospects & Capstone Project
Weeks: 9–10 | Topics: 6 | Grade: 35% | Time: ~26 hrs
5.1 Global CCS Deployment Scenarios

Scaling up CCS 100-fold to meet net-zero targets by 2050.

5.2 Policy, Economics, and Carbon Pricing

Carbon markets (ETS), tax credits (45Q), and subsidies driving deployment.

5.3 Carbon Credit Markets and Monetisation

Voluntary and compliance markets, registries (Verra, Gold Standard), and offtake agreements.

5.4 CO&sub2; Utilisation

Converting captured carbon into e-fuels, concrete, or chemicals.

5.5 CCS in Rural Contexts (India Focus)

Applying aggregation models and DMRV in Indian agriculture.

5.6 Capstone Project

Develop a complete Carbon Project Proposal covering: Baseline Assessment, Carbon Accounting, MRV Plan, Financial Model, and Offtake Strategy.

8–10 hours per week for 10 weeks, self-paced within each module. You can start any topic in any order within your enrolled modules and revisit materials any time.
Are there any prerequisites?
A bachelor's degree or equivalent professional experience is preferred. Basic familiarity with climate change is helpful but not required — Module 1 covers all foundational content.
Is the course fully online?
Yes, fully online with recorded lectures, readings, interactive quizzes, and assignments. Optional monthly live Q&A sessions via Zoom are recorded and made available to all enrolled students.

No. Just a web browser and internet connection. The financial model template uses Excel; the MRV data form is a PDF. Both are optional tools provided as downloadable templates.
